# Information Classification and Protection
## Sanitized Historical Policy Excerpt

**Portfolio:** UBT-CERT establishment and leadership case study  
**Historical role:** Atdhe Buja, CERT Manager  
**Source documents:** 2.1 Information Classification; 2.2 Information Protection  
**Portfolio adaptation prepared:** September 2026

## Context and Contribution

As CERT Manager, I drafted and implemented information classification and protection policies as part of UBT-CERT's governance development and international membership preparation.

The selected material illustrates how the policy assigned responsibility for information, defined access expectations, protected third-party information, and connected information categories with protection levels.

## Editorial Scope

This is a sanitized, paraphrased excerpt of selected provisions, not a verbatim original or a complete operational policy. Wording has been edited for clarity, institution-specific approval details and contact information omitted, and the category table condensed. The original category names and protection levels are retained.

The surviving classification document contains an incomplete date. No exact policy issue date or signed approval is asserted by this adaptation. This page documents historical policy design rather than current UBT-CERT procedures.

## Selected Policy Provisions

### 1. Individual Access and Accountability

Access to CERT systems is provided through individually assigned user identifiers and passwords. Users may access only resources for which they are authorized. Passwords are confidential and must not be shared. Users are accountable for activity performed under their assigned accounts.

### 2. Information Ownership and Availability

Information owners are responsible for classifying and securing information within their area of responsibility. Information must be protected against unauthorized access while remaining available to authorized users carrying out their duties.

### 3. Classification and Protection Levels

| Historical Category | Protection Level | Selected Handling Principles |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Public information (Non-sensitive) | Standard | Information approved for public release; internet transmission and discussion permitted under standard security measures. |
| Sensitive (Internal Business use only data) | Mid-level | Restricted handling of client and internal information; authorized, read-only access described in the protection matrix; changes to original client information require written permission. |
| Confidential | High level | Restricted access and physical and technical protection; the matrix describes access limited to a small number of people for a limited time. |

These are the historical framework's labels and summarized controls. They are not a complete modern control baseline or a mapping to a current information-sharing classification standard.

### 4. Third-Party Information

Third-party information is to be used for the purpose for which it was collected, protected throughout its lifetime, and disclosed only with appropriate consent or authorization. The source policy calls for lawful and fair collection, retention tied to relevance and applicable requirements, and protection through access controls or secured physical storage.

### 5. Employment Departure

On termination of employment, information is to be archived or deleted according to the applicable records-retention policy. This excerpt does not reproduce the separate retention schedule.

## What This Sample Demonstrates

- Translating information protection goals into user responsibilities and handling rules.
- Connecting classification with differentiated protection measures.
- Addressing both electronic records and physical information.
- Recognizing confidentiality obligations in collaboration with external parties.

## Historical Limitation

The source classification table includes information received from FIRST teams under both Sensitive and Confidential examples, without a rule resolving the overlap. This condensed sample does not reproduce those examples. An operational revision would need an explicit classification decision rule before use.

The source references ISO/IEC 27001 and ISO/IEC 27002. This is a historical reference to the policy's stated basis, not evidence of organizational certification or a verified control-by-control mapping.
